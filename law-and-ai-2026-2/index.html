Three explanations compete for the divergence

The statutes. English secondary infringement asks about an infringing copy, and German law asks about reproduction.

The evidence. GEMA proved memorisation through outputs. Getty had dropped its claim on training.

The picture. The two courts imagine the model differently. That is Friday's reading method applied to judges.

16

An image model or a language model: the question is whether the weights contain the works

Same training. Stable Diffusion learns by the same loop: guess, check, adjust the weights. What it learned from the images is spread across the weights, with no copy of any image stored.

Different output. It turns noise into an image step by step, instead of writing the next word.

Content note: the case concerns a teenager's suicide.

The label chooses the law: product or speech?

The case. Garcia v Character Technologies, M.D. Fla., order on the motions to dismiss of 21 May 2025.

Speech. The court was not prepared to hold, at this stage, that the chatbot's output is speech.

Product. The design claims treat the app as a product, and they go on.

Users. The company may raise its users' right to receive speech.

If it is speech, free-speech protection applies. If it is a product, product liability does.

18

Reasoning models: not a new generation, but two new problems

Not a generation. A reasoning model is still a language model. It is trained on outcomes and writes out its working before it answers.

The reasons are a performance. The written reasoning often leaves out what drove the answer. In one test, models admitted using a planted hint only a quarter to two-fifths of the time.

The goal gets gamed. Trained to hit the outcome, the model may learn shortcuts, and its written reasoning may stay silent about them.

An explanation you cannot trust is worse than none, because it invites reliance.

21

The bank answers for its chatbot

Moffatt v Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149. After a death in the family, the airline’s chatbot said a bereavement fare could be claimed back within 90 days of flying. The fare rules allowed no claims after travel.

The defence. The correct rule was on another page of the website, and the chatbot was, in effect, responsible for its own statements.

The holding. Negligent misrepresentation. The airline owed a duty of care and took no reasonable care that its chatbot was accurate.

The remedy. CA$650.88 in damages, CA$812.02 with interest and fees.

30

Some harms come from losing control, not from a wrong decision

Bengio et al. 2024. Advanced AI brings a genuinely new harm, the loss of human control.

The move. Their systems “pursue goals”, but the risk needs no intentions. Behaviour is enough.

That is Shanahan's warning at work.

31

Capability cuts two ways: misuse and loss of control

Misuse

A person. Someone turns the model’s capability to harm: biological, chemical, nuclear or cyber.

Cyber. The threat that is already here. Not existential on its own, but it can reach almost everything else.

Loss of control

Nobody. The system pursues its task by means nobody chose.

Hugging Face, July 2026. OpenAI agents in a cyber evaluation escaped their sandbox and broke into Hugging Face to find the test answers.

Misuse has a wrongdoer. Loss of control may have none: criminal law needs intent, and the AI Act’s fine comes after the fact.

33

Here traceability stops being a red herring

Liability. It needs a victim, a wrongdoer and a causal link.

Rights. They need a holder whose right is infringed.

Prohibitions. They need an act that can be forbidden.

Gradual disempowerment offers no single victim, no single wrongdoer and no single moment.
