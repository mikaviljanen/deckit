11

Legal capability is built by heavy lifting, 
at university and at work

At university

Knowledge has come first, and capability has followed. We mostly monitored the outputs only.

At work

Capability leads, and knowledge keeps accumulating. Bosses, clients and courts watched closely.

But the machines are taking the heavy lifting in both places.

12

It never gets easier. You just go faster.

Greg LeMond (or so the story goes)

13

We need the lifting, so we need a gym

You go there, you sweat, you get sore. Later you work just to stay where you are.

Sessions. Two or three hours, many of them. People read, write and argue in a room together.

The gym is not enough. Machines for the extra hours: a trainer that never gets tired.

It cannot be just AI. Somebody has to make sure that the AI is a useful trainer.

14

Four things to build, starting last month

The space

Rooms and hours where students do the work themselves, counted in the degree.

Human feedback

Teachers and fellow students read the work and answer it, often and closely.

AI buddies

For the extra hours. They ask questions, and they are purpose-built and checked.

The proof

We need to see students do law, in the room and without the machine.

And when do we start?
