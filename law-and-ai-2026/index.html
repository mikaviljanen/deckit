6

In pairs: what changed between Text A and Text B?

Text A · Commission proposal, April 2021

Art. 3(1). ‘Artificial intelligence system’ (AI system) means software that is developed with one or more of the techniques and approaches listed in Annex I and can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, generate outputs such as content, predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing the environments they interact with.

Annex I. (a) Machine learning approaches, including supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning, using a wide variety of methods including deep learning; (b) Logic- and knowledge-based approaches, including knowledge representation, inductive (logic) programming, knowledge bases, inference and deductive engines, (symbolic) reasoning and expert systems; (c) Statistical approaches, Bayesian estimation, search and optimization methods.

Text B · AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

Art. 3(1). ‘AI system’ means a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments.

8

The AI changed under the legislator, and so did the politics

2022. The Council narrowed the definition to machine learning and logic-based systems, for fear of catching ordinary software. It drafted the cut before ChatGPT and adopted it a week after.

2023. The OECD widened its definition in November to cover generative AI, and the Act copied the OECD wording.

So the definition dates itself, and we date every text.

Two myths

Both sit in the AI Act's definition.

24

AI does not learn while you use it

Training. The weights change with every batch, until the version ships.

Use. The weights are frozen, and everyone gets the same model.

Memory is notes fed back into the prompt.

Older systems. They are retrained in nightly or weekly batches, and a person decides when.

The Act. Art. 3(1) still says that a system “may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment”.

25

Autonomy and opacity are different things

Chat assistant. A human reads every answer.

Agent. It runs for hours unattended.

Expert system. A clerk signs every decision.

Thermostat. It is fully readable and fully autonomous.

Opacity

Autonomy

The dial. Whoever builds the harness decides where a human must approve.

The fog. Opacity makes behaviour look willful. Its goals come from training and the prompt.
